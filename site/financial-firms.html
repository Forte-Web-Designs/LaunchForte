01 / Where it breaks

Four places a firm loses time and risks errors.

The money work is manual.

Invoices, capital calls, billing, and reconciliation are done by hand, which is slow and one typo away from a problem.

Your systems don't talk.

Your books, your CRM, your forms, and your spreadsheets each hold a piece, and someone re-keys between them.

You can't fully trust the numbers.

Reporting is stitched together by hand, so the picture is always a little behind and never quite certain.

There's no clean audit trail.

When the work moves money, you need to show what happened and when, and a pile of manual steps can't.

When a system moves money, reliability is the whole job. I spent years keeping that kind of system up at a national bank.

02 / The stack

One connected system, with the controls the work demands.

This is the engine a financial or professional firm runs on, from intake to a clean audit trail.

Intake.

Client and investor onboarding forms (ex: Jotform), wired into the CRM and the books so nothing gets re-keyed.

The CRM, your source of truth.

HubSpot or Airtable, every client, deal, investor, and document in one place.

Financial automation.

Invoicing, capital calls, billing, and collections follow up, run through QuickBooks, with the steps that need a human kept in human hands.

Data pipelines with controls.

The reporting and data work automated end to end, with no auto execute anywhere money or trades are involved.

Reporting and audit trail.

One dashboard for the numbers, and a clean record of what ran, when, on whose approval, and with what result.

Reliability and monitoring.

Hardened credentials and monitoring, with alerts when something looks off, so you always know the system is doing what it should.
