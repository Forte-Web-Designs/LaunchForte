The Lead Capture Audit Worksheet

A self assessment that surfaces the gaps in how you capture and route leads, before they cost you another deal.

More guides

Why most lead problems are not lead problems

Most businesses believe they have a lead volume problem. The gap is usually upstream of that, in how leads get captured and routed, and it stays invisible because you never see the lead that filled out a form that emailed a dead inbox, or the one that called after hours and got voicemail, or the one that sat unassigned for three days until it went cold. You paid to generate every one of those, and then lost them before a human ever said hello. This worksheet walks the path a lead takes from the moment it arrives to the moment a person follows up, and surfaces every place it can be lost.

Answer each question for your own business as it works today. Mark a check for yes and leave it blank for no. The blanks are your gaps, and the scoring at the end tells you how much is slipping through.

Section 1, Capture coverage

Does every entry point land in a system, or do some die where they arrive?

  • Every website form writes directly into your CRM or database, so the lead exists as a record.
  • Inbound phone calls are logged as leads with the caller's number and the reason they called.
  • Social and direct message inquiries are captured into the same system as everything else.
  • Leads from referrals, events, and partners get entered the same day, by a process you can rely on.
  • Paid ad leads flow straight into the system with the campaign that produced them attached.

Section 2, Capture quality

When a lead is captured, do you get enough to route and personalize it?

  • Every lead record includes the source, so you know which channel it came from.
  • You capture enough context to know what the lead wants, alongside their name and contact.
  • The data arrives clean and structured, ready to act on without anyone retyping it.
  • A new record is complete enough that the right person could act on it without asking the lead to repeat themselves.

Section 3, Speed to lead

How fast does a new lead hear from you?

  • A new lead gets an automatic acknowledgement within minutes, day or night.
  • A first human response reaches the lead in under an hour during business hours.
  • Leads that arrive after hours or on weekends are handled, rather than parked until Monday.
  • You know your average time to first response, as a number, right now.

Section 4, Routing and assignment

Once captured, does a lead reach the right person without someone sorting it by hand?

  • A captured lead is assigned to the right owner or pipeline automatically.
  • There is a defined fallback if the assigned owner does not respond within a set time.
  • Routing accounts for the lead's source, location, or type, so the right specialist gets it.
  • No lead waits in a shared inbox for someone to notice it and decide where it goes.

Section 5, One source of truth

Can you see every lead in one place?

  • Every lead, from every channel, lives in one system you can open right now.
  • You hold no separate spreadsheets or inboxes that contain leads the main system does not.
  • A lead's status reads the same wherever you look, with no conflicting versions.
  • You could pull a list of every lead from the last 30 days in under a minute.

Section 6, Follow up and fallthrough

What happens to a lead that does not reply the first time?

  • A lead that does not respond enters an automated multi touch follow up.
  • Follow up runs on its own schedule and does not depend on someone remembering.
  • Leads that go cold are re engaged later by design.
  • You can see, for any lead, how many times you have reached out and when.

Section 7, Measurement

Do you know what your capture is doing?

  • You know your overall capture rate, the share of inquiries that become tracked leads.
  • You can tell which sources produce leads that become revenue.
  • You can see where leads drop off between arriving and the first conversation.
  • Your numbers come from the system itself, with nobody tallying by hand.

Score yourself

Count your checks out of 30.

  • 27 to 30. Solid. Your capture and routing hold together. Put your attention on speed and on which sources convert.
  • 20 to 26. Gaps. A few clear ones are costing you leads every week. Close the lowest scoring sections first.
  • 12 to 19. Bleeding. You are losing a meaningful share of what you generate before anyone works it. This is the highest return thing on your list.
  • Under 12. Your capture is held together by people and memory, and it will not survive more volume. Start with one source of truth and automatic routing.

The fixes that matter most, in order

The order is deliberate, because each one is wasted without the one before it. First, get every lead into one system automatically, since you cannot route or measure what you did not capture. Second, route and acknowledge instantly, because speed to lead moves conversion more than anything else on this page. Third, automate the follow up, since most replies come after the first touch and that is where the abandoned leads sit. Fourth, put measurement on top, so you can watch the gaps close and find the next one.
